# Research Report Template

Use this structure for the final report. Adapt headings to the topic, but keep the order: reader gets the answer first, then the support, then the caveats.

## Structure

1. **Title and metadata line**: report title, date of research, and the depth (for example "23 searches, 14 sources read in full").

2. **Executive summary** (roughly half a page)
   - The bottom-line answer to the user's question in 2 to 4 sentences.
   - 3 to 6 key findings, each with a confidence tag (High / Medium / Low).
   - The single most important caveat.

3. **Question and scope**
   - The question as you understood it, the scope, and any assumptions you made. This lets the reader catch a misunderstanding early.

4. **Method** (short)
   - The sub-questions you broke it into, the rounds of searching, and how you judged sources. Mention where the plan changed and why, since that shows how the conclusion was reached.

5. **Findings by sub-question**
   - One section per sub-question. For each: the answer, the evidence (paraphrased, with source names and links), and a confidence tag.
   - Use a table where comparing options or figures is clearer than prose.

6. **Conflicting evidence and uncertainty**
   - Where credible sources disagree, what the disagreement turns on, and which side you find more convincing and why (or that you cannot tell).
   - Claims that rest on a single source or old data.

7. **Gaps and limitations**
   - What you looked for and could not find, what would need primary data or an expert, and any limits on the search (paywalls, language, recency).

8. **Conclusions and next steps**
   - What follows from the evidence. If the user needs to decide something, lay out the options and trade-offs. Give recommendations only where the evidence supports them, and mark judgment calls as judgment.

9. **Sources**
   - Numbered list: title, publisher, date, URL. Group by primary and secondary if that helps. Only list sources actually used.

## Writing guidance

- Lead with the answer. Do not make the reader wade through method to find it.
- Prefer specific, checkable statements over vague summaries. "Revenue rose 12% in FY2025 according to the company's annual filing" beats "revenue grew significantly".
- Tag confidence where a claim matters; do not tag trivia.
- Keep prose tight. Depth comes from evidence and reasoning, not length. Use headings, short paragraphs, and tables for scanning.
- Cite inline by source name or number so each finding can be traced. No invented citations.
- Write in plain language, and define specialist terms the first time they appear.
